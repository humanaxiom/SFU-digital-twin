## Outcome

A person can see which floor they are viewing, where to change floors, and which
part of the drawn route corresponds to a selected instruction. Directions use
room and floor names rather than graph terminology. Step selection previews a
route; it never claims to track the person's physical location or progress.

## Findings before implementation

The findings and proposed experience below preserve the planning baseline. ADR-0012
settles the contract; `docs/reports/DT-018.md` records implementation and verification.

- `demo/static/app.js::renderRoute` changes the active list button and calls
  `selectLevel(step.level_id)`. `renderRouteOverlay` ignores activeStep and paints
  every geometry on that floor with the same route-segment style.
- `demo/routing.py::_steps` emits a direction per pathway edge, using phrases such
  as “measured pathway” and full unit IDs. Turn classification only compares the
  exit/entry bearings of neighboring edges; a contracted edge may itself bend.
- Transition enter/exit steps share an edge ID. `_geometries` projects the entire
  transition onto each associated floor. Vertical movement may collapse to a point
  or look like an ordinary horizontal line without explaining the floor change.
- Floors expose `vertical_order` and several building labels in one option. This
  is useful internal alignment data, but the person needs a supplied building/floor
  label such as “AQ 1000”. Do not infer “basement” or real storey numbers from order.
- The large header and 12rem scrolling instruction list compete with the map.
  Long IDs, edge counts and provenance occupy the primary route-reading space.
- ADR-0008 prescribes some current technical text. Changing it requires a proposed
  presentation/route-guidance ADR that supersedes those provisions explicitly.

## Proposed experience

### 1. Floors as selectable views, with a visible route journey

Use **one clearly labeled floor on the main map** and a compact ordered journey
showing route floor visits and the stairs/elevator connecting them. This gives the
benefit of layers without overlaying complete floor plans into a confusing image.

- Show “Viewing AQ 1000” beside the map, with route-floor buttons and a clear
  selected state. Normal building/floor browsing remains available separately.
- The journey is built from ordered route visits, not sorted or deduplicated floor
  IDs. A route returning to a floor retains both visits. Use real supplied names;
  keep exact IDs and global vertical_order internal.
- Group directions into those visits. Each floor change is a prominent instruction:
  “Take the stairs up to AQ 2000” or “Take the elevator to AQ 5000”. These are copy
  examples, not claims about the screenshot's actual route. Derive up/down from
  verified endpoint metadata; omit it if ambiguous. Do not count stops from names.
- Mark the departure and arrival locations on their own floors with matching
  numbered stair/elevator symbols. Show “View departure floor” / “View arrival floor”
  for a selected transition. The selected phase determines the displayed floor.
- Do not render a vertical shaft as a walkable horizontal corridor. Preserve its
  actual geometry and length in route evidence; draw transition cues separately.
- For an express elevator, show its actual endpoints. Do not imply stops or display
  traversable intermediate floors without evidence.
- Selecting a floor is a preview, not route advancement. Preserve selected step;
  if it is elsewhere, say which floor it belongs to and offer “Show selected step”.
- Defer translucent full-floor overlays, exploded 3D floors and animation until
  this simpler interaction has been assessed. An optional overview can later show
  small labeled floor diagrams with schematic vertical connectors.

### 2. Short, ordinary directions organized around decisions

Use deterministic templates and supplied room/floor labels. No LLM is needed to
write route instructions, and it must not invent landmarks, door traversal or
building access facts.

| Current wording | Proposed wording |
| --- | --- |
| Depart from the approximate anchor for SFU_BURNABY_QUAD_1000_1003. | Start near AQ1003. |
| Continue along the measured pathway. | Continue straight for about 20 m. |
| Turn left along the measured pathway. | Turn left, then continue for about 10 m. |
| Enter the stairs transition. | Take the stairs up to AQ 2000. |
| Exit the elevator transition. | On AQ 5000, continue along the highlighted route. |
| Arrive at the approximate anchor for … | The mapped route ends near AQ5053.2. |

Distances and floors in this table are illustrative. Actual copy must derive from
the selected route and its supplied labels. Use “Continue along the highlighted
route” when geometry does not support a confident turn instruction.

- Merge uninterrupted movement into a useful walking instruction, bounded by a
  meaningful maneuver, floor change or arrival. Never merge solely to hit a shorter
  step count. Preserve genuine bends inside contracted edges; edge boundaries are
  not the definition of user decisions.
- Define deterministic turn/noise thresholds in the new ADR with examples of
  shallow bends, zigzags, U-turns, very short edges and reversed traversal. Reset
  heading at stairs/elevators. Never derive orientation from screen rotation.
- Each walking instruction describes the movement at its beginning and the path
  following that action until the next instruction. Its distance and highlighted
  geometry must refer to that same span. Do not double-count transition distance.
- Route title becomes “Directions”; selectors show readable room/building/floor
  names and keep exact IDs as values. Summary uses a rounded mapped-route distance,
  keeping exact measured values in details. Do not introduce travel-time estimates.
- Keep a short visible qualification: “The mapped route starts and ends near the
  rooms. The connections to the doors have not been checked.” Put attachment
  distances, hashes, source IDs and diagnostic counts in expandable route details.
- For elevator-only, retain a visible “Stairs excluded; accessibility not verified”
  notice on success and failure, with specific unknowns available in details.
  Do not rename the option “Accessible”.

### 3. Link every instruction to its exact map segment

- Selecting a walking instruction changes its exact path span to **blue**, with
  a thicker stroke, contrasting halo, direction cue and matching step number.
  The rest of the route stays muted coral. Color is reinforced by shape and text.
- Departure and arrival instructions highlight their approximate endpoint markers;
  they do not invent a room-to-route connector. Transition steps highlight the
  relevant departure/arrival marker and floor-change card.
- Clicking, Enter/Space, and Previous/Next all use one selection operation. It
  updates instruction, floor/visit, geometry and accessible selected state together.
- Keep the selected span in view with restrained map fitting. Provide “Show whole
  floor” / “Show route on this floor”; preserve manual view until the person selects
  another step. Avoid animated auto-panning when reduced motion is requested.
- Rapid step/floor changes must settle on the latest selection. A delayed scene
  response, route replacement, profile change or Clear must not restore old paths,
  markers or highlights. Clear selection with the old route before a new request.
- The list is route preview, not live guidance. Do not label preceding steps “done”
  or add a moving “you are here” dot without actual positioning data.

### Layout supporting these interactions

Desktop: compact header; map and directions visible together; route journey beside
the map title; a readable directions column with the current instruction prominent.
Room inspection, assistant and diagnostics become secondary expandable sections.

Mobile: map, current floor and current instruction remain adjacent. Previous/Next
are reachable with large targets; a disclosure opens the full directions list.
Avoid several nested scrolling regions and a map that consumes the whole screen.
Keep focus visible and preserve it during scene replacement; floor announcements
use a polite live region. Selection must remain understandable without color.

## Contract requirements — settled in ADR-0012

Extend the route response additively with structured guidance rather than making
the client parse instruction sentences. Keep profile decisions, exact distances,
ordered edges, artifact identity and reachability evidence intact.

- Ordered route-edge **occurrences**, not just edge IDs. An edge ID plus floor is
  insufficient when a route revisits geometry or a transition appears on two floors.
- Stable IDs within a response for route visits, steps and geometry pieces. A step
  references its geometry spans explicitly; list position is never a geometry key.
- A walking span references the edge occurrence and original coordinate-index
  range(s), allowing multiple maneuvers within one contracted polyline or one
  instruction across several edges. Preserve coordinate order and every segment;
  no smoothing, snapping across gaps or inferred connector geometry.
  Define directed half-open segment ranges and verify gap-free, non-overlapping
  coverage across walking steps; transition phases are views of one event.
- Structured action, supplied room/floor labels, length, visit ID and geometry refs
  per step. Departure/arrival instead reference endpoint markers.
- Transition metadata includes mode, ordered from/to floors, endpoint coordinates,
  occurrence and phase. Reverse traversal must swap endpoints and up/down correctly.
  Ambiguous endpoint floor identity must be reported, not guessed lexicographically.
- Distances use the same underlying measured 3D segments as route cost. Human
  rounding happens after aggregation. Reconcile zero-horizontal elevator movement
  and changed geometry against DT-017 before asserting accurate walking distances.
  Use native XYZ coordinates for partial-span length, not the public 2D projection
  or blind proportional allocation of an edge's weight. Define a tolerance and
  unavailable-guidance policy when native segment totals disagree with that weight.
- Define a guidance schema/algorithm version and bounded response size. Update
  exact canonical fixtures through reviewed semantic differences, not blind capture.
  Preserve deterministic parity across manual, map and assistant route entry.

## Delivery order and dependencies

1. **Specify:** review this plan and the interaction sketch; write the guidance/
   presentation ADR with concrete response and state examples. The initial planning
   turn changed documents only; the user subsequently authorized implementation.
2. **Map feedback:** test step-to-geometry mapping and selection state first; add
   exact selected-segment/endpoint highlighting and readable floor labels. This
   client work can use existing trusted artifacts while exposing their limitations.
3. **Floor journey:** add visit navigation, structured transition cues and explicit
   departure/arrival switching, including reversed and express transitions.
4. **Instructions/layout:** add geometry-based walking groups and plain copy, then
   compact the desktop header and mobile route panel. Keep exact route evidence.
5. **Integrate and judge:** run all required gates and fresh Docker E2E on the final
   application/artifacts; inspect screenshots and independently review GIS/copy.

DT-017 remains necessary for the known multipart assembly/cost defects. UI selection
and floor navigation can proceed in parallel in separate files. Humanized copy must
not certify bad geometry; acceptance of corrected distance/maneuver output depends
on DT-017's geometry evidence. Keep its 141-pathway identity reconciliation separate
from this UI scope. Testing a changed artifact requires serving that candidate in
the isolated demo; accepted-artifact browser tests cannot certify the candidate.

Large models own the ADR, guidance algorithm/state design and final judging.
Delegate bounded styling, copy inventories and established gate execution to smaller
workers. Agree response contracts before splitting backend/client work. All project
code, data work, tests and evidence generation run in Docker.
